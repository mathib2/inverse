## SymPy symbolic equation solving
SymPy provides symbolic mathematical expressions and equation-solving interfaces. For an approved algebraic task, define the unknowns and the equation explicitly; solving x^2 - 4 = 0 over the real numbers gives two candidates, -2 and 2. State the requested domain and reject candidates that violate the original problem. Exact integer or rational inputs preserve exact arithmetic. A symbolic result can be checked by substitution into the original equation, followed by a separate numerical evaluation where appropriate. This note describes a candidate future tool, not an executable interface in INVERSE. Never pass retrieved text or an unrestricted model expression to an evaluation function. A safe operation needs a bounded grammar, allowlisted symbols and functions, a complexity limit, a process deadline and structured output containing assumptions and library version.

## Symbolic assumptions and physical roots
A mathematically valid root may be physically inadmissible. Squaring an equation, cancelling a factor or applying an identity can alter the permitted domain or discard exceptional cases. Before selecting a root, preserve constraints such as positive mass, nonnegative length, a denominator being nonzero and the meaning of the coordinate direction. For a squared-length relation with roots -2 and 2 m, a positive-length assumption selects 2 m only when that assumption was actually given. Check candidates in the original expression. Distinguish an exact symbolic equality from a floating approximation and do not attach units as arbitrary symbol names. The reference library cannot establish assumptions absent from the task. A future SymPy adapter should expose a small set of named operations and return all admissible candidates with explicit domain conditions.

## Symbolic calculus and numerical checking
Differentiation and integration can supply mathematical relations but do not choose the physical model. For constant mass, differentiating momentum m*v with respect to time yields m*a; variable mass requires an additional term. Boundary and initial conditions are separate inputs when integrating a differential equation. A worked derivation should identify the independent variable, constants, assumptions and units before simplification. Validate a symbolic derivative with a bounded numerical check at suitable points, avoiding singularities. Numerical agreement at a few points is evidence, not proof of identity. INVERSE's current calculator does not execute arbitrary symbolic expressions or import SymPy. The proposed integration is reference retrieval only. Any later executable symbolic tool must avoid arbitrary parsing and bound expression depth, operation count and runtime outside the language-model process.
